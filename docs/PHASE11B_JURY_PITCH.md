# SIGNAL — Three-minute challenge-owner jury pitch

**ACAPS Ground Truth | Hackathon for Good #8**

Target: ~3 minutes **including** a short, preloaded live demonstration. Assign speakers and practice aloud with the actual screen transitions. The official ACAPS challenge brief specifies a **3-minute pitch**, even if general event guidance mentions a different format.

## 0:00–0:25 — The missing feedback loop

“ACAPS produces vital humanitarian risk analysis. But valuable ground-level knowledge often stays in informal conversations. A field expert might disagree with a risk, notice a missing factor, or see conditions changing—without an easy, trusted way to feed that insight back. The challenge is to make those contributions fast, useful and safe.”

**On screen:** Risk Explorer, Chad highlighted; open R1043. **Do not pause to explain every chart.**

## 0:25–0:55 — What SIGNAL is

“This is **SIGNAL**: a human-in-the-loop, moderator-first expert forecasting system. We start from an actual ACAPS Risk Radar record, distinguish the current humanitarian context from a specific six-month forecast question, and invite a small trusted panel to independently assess it.”

**On screen:** R1043 source context and concise expert Brief/Call view. State that panel members are synthetic.

## 0:55–1:35 — The Delphi engine

“Experts contribute a numeric event probability, their agreement or disagreement, and one short missing factor. Their initial judgments are independent. Once the panel is complete, a moderator decides when anonymized group feedback is released. Experts can then keep or revise their estimate. The system shows disagreement rather than hiding it.”

**On screen:** Prepared Moderator Studio: initial and revised panels; release/approval states. If already approved, **describe** the sequence instead of pretending to publish live.

## 1:35–2:15 — Evidence and human control

“Our moderator workspace connects the original ACAPS assessment, source context, and the panel's evidence gaps. The review queue surfaces missing source fields without inventing facts or silently changing risk ratings. A human moderator reviews the result and approves a frozen, group-level forecast. Our visual characters explain these roles; they are not five autonomous AI agents.”

**On screen:** Evidence review queue, then approved snapshot state.

## 2:15–2:45 — One click, two privacy levels

“Now we switch to the **Public Situation Room**. Individual estimates and notes are not returned. Partners receive a coarse, approved group band with transparent limitations. The INFORM Severity context is a separate official measure of an existing crisis—not a prediction. Our What-if lab is explicitly a simulation.”

**On screen:** Analyst/Public switch. Demonstrate no raw expert notes. Show approved public forecast band.

## 2:45–3:00 — Why it matters

“SIGNAL makes expert engagement structured, reviewable and potentially repeatable—without replacing ACAPS judgment. Our next step is a small supervised pilot with real consent and security, configurable consultations, and verified outcome scoring. **Better evidence, faster expert feedback, human decisions.**”

## Q&A facts / concise answers

- **Are these real experts?** No, every invitation and contribution is synthetic.
- **Are these validated probabilities?** No. They are descriptive aggregates of hypothetical expert judgments; future empirical calibration requires resolved outcomes.
- **Did AI write the analysis?** The working core is deterministic. Agent Crew characters are explanatory, not autonomous production agents.
- **Can users be identified?** Demo public endpoints limit released information, but the fixed-code prototype is not safe for real private contributors.
- **Where did the data come from?** Organizer-provided ACAPS Risk Radar; crisis-level INFORM Severity releases, used as contextual information.
- **Can ACAPS run this tomorrow?** An analyst can inspect the local synthetic prototype. A real pilot would require proper security, consent, configurable panels and reviewed data governance.
