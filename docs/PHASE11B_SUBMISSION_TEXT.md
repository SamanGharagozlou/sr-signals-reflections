# SIGNAL — Junction submission text

## Project title

**SIGNAL — Ground Truth & Humanitarian Forecasting**

## One-line description

A trusted, moderator-led expert loop that turns humanitarian risk assessments into transparent, privacy-conscious event forecasts and decision-ready group feedback.

## Short description (copy into the submission form)

SIGNAL helps humanitarian analysts structure the expert knowledge that often disappears into informal conversations. Built for the ACAPS Ground Truth challenge, our local-first prototype links source-grounded ACAPS Risk Radar records with independent synthetic expert forecasts. A small panel responds to a specific six-month event question; after moderator approval, experts see anonymized group feedback and may revise their views. ACAPS-style analysts can inspect uncertainty, evidence gaps and discrepancies, then approve a frozen, privacy-limited public summary. SIGNAL also visualizes the wider risk catalogue and contextual INFORM Severity data without confusing existing crisis conditions with future-event probabilities. The prototype uses deterministic aggregation, explicit human checkpoints and separate analyst/public API boundaries. The demo uses fictional experts and is not a live ACAPS service, calibrated prediction model, or production-ready system.

## What makes it distinctive

- **Short, structured expert participation** instead of an AI chatbot.
- **Two-step Delphi feedback loop** that preserves independent first judgments and makes dissent visible to moderators.
- **Human-controlled output:** publication and counterpoint releases require moderator action.
- **Traceable context:** Risk Radar source, official severity context, missing-field review, and bounded interpretation.
- **Public privacy boundary:** published group band only, not expert notes or individual forecasts.
- **A memorable visual experience:** Risk Explorer, Situation Room, and Rita's illustration-led Agent Crew.
